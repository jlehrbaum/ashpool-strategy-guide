Start Here: What Every Player Should Know

Wing Fighter looks like a game about collecting stronger equipment and increasing your Battle Power (BP). But as you progress, you'll discover that how you build matters just as much as how much you build.

A player with lower BP can beat someone much stronger by investing in the right stats, combining equipment effectively, and exploiting weaknesses in their opponent's setup.

This guide explains how to do that. It's a deep dive into the mechanics, strategies, and investment decisions that make the biggest difference in both PVE (fighting the game) and PVP (fighting other players). If you're new to these systems, the eight principles below are the most important things to understand before diving into the detailed sections.

1. BP isn't everything

Higher Battle Power doesn't guarantee victory. A well-optimized 30M player can beat a poorly optimized 45M player. Your stats, equipment combinations, and ability to counter an opponent often matter more than the number next to your name.

2. Your systems work together

Four systems carry most build-level choices:

  • Chip Formations: three saved stat combinations you swap between before fights.
  • Sentinels: abilities and stacking passive bonuses.
  • Mech Spirits: combat skills, attribute bonuses, and collection-level combination bonuses.
  • Engines: one configurable stat boost per fight.

The real power is in how they reinforce each other. A defensive build combines Dodge, heal, and damage reduction. A crit build combines Crit Rate, Hit Rate, and Crit Damage.

3. Every build has a counter

No single build wins every matchup. Your job is to strengthen whichever stats your opponent is least equipped to counter.

  • Crit works against low Crit Resistance.
  • Dodge works against low Hit Rate.
  • Burn bypasses Dodge entirely, which makes it a direct dodge-build counter.

4. Pilots are your foundation

Pilots drive four stats: Physical Defense Break (PDB), Energy Defense Break (EDB), Physical Defense (PD), and Energy Defense (ED). These work on a multiplier, a strong pilot advantage multiplies your damage, and a deficit shrinks it. If pilots fall behind, no amount of equipment upgrades will make up the gap. Don't neglect pilots while chasing new gear, and keep both defensive stats (PD and ED) competitive even if your offense is specialized.

5. Know the difference between raw stats and effective stats

Most of the game's important caps are net, applied after your opponent's counters. Burn Enhancement caps at 100% effective:

  • PVE: 100% raw hits the cap.
  • PVP vs. 100% Burn Reduction: you need 200% raw to deliver the same 100% effective.

Crit and Dodge work the same way, with 80% effective caps in PVP. A stat above its advertised cap isn't automatically wasted, what matters is what's left after the opponent counters it.

6. Focus your investments

Resources are limited (especially f2p). Building everything at once gives you several mediocre builds instead of one or two strong ones. Reasonable starting commitments:

  • A PVE Burn setup for bosses.
  • A PVP offense or defense that fits your play style.
  • Max accessible drones and sentinels before chasing higher rarities.
  • Mech combination bonuses, since they pay out across the whole collection.

7. Your strategy should change as you progress

Some upgrades don't just increase a number, they unlock a new build. The clearest example is 100% native Burn Enhancement, hitting 100% without any burn chips. Before you're there, you need a dedicated PVE burn formation. After, your Crit/Hit formation doubles as PVE, which frees up a slot for a dedicated PVP Burn build.

8. Learn why a build works before copying it

Recommended builds here are starting points, not universal answers. A build that crushes for one player may underperform for another because pilots, drones, resources, or likely opponents are different. Figure out what each piece is doing (damage, survival, countering something, enabling something else) and you can swap when the matchup calls for it.

Part 1: Understanding the Fundamentals

Part 1 is about the thinking behind strong builds, before any specific stat or system. Build Philosophy covers how builds counter each other and where to invest your attention. Pilots covers the foundation stat that everything else sits on top of.

Build Philosophy: How to Think About Builds

The game has way more possible builds than you can afford to tune, especially f2p. Three ideas drive the rest of this section: every build has a counter, the layers you optimize matter more than your raw power level, and pairing a primary attribute with the right secondaries is what makes a build actually win.

Every Build Has a Counter

There is no single dominant build. Every offensive style is vulnerable to a defensive style, and every defense has an attacker that can crack it. The strongest players accept this and aim for one of two outcomes:

  • Specialize the secondaries: Pair your primary attribute with the secondaries that enable it. Dodge needs heal to outlast enemies. Crit needs hit (so you can land attacks) and crit damage (so the crits matter). The combinations that win are dodge + heal, or crit + hit + crit damage. A build that stacks one primary attribute without its enablers is weak.
  • Overpower the primaries: Push your primary attribute high enough that the opponent's secondary defenses can't keep up. This is the brute-force path and tends to require deeper investment, but it's effective when specialization isn't an option.

The Layer Hierarchy

Layers in order of impact. Investing well in a higher layer beats investing more in a lower one.

4. Drone / sentinel / mech optimizationLanding the right passives, equipping the right onesHigh-impact tuning. Gold passives can multiply effectiveness in any of the layers above.

Base stats at the bottom surprises most players, since that's where the big visible numbers live. But a maxed-gear opponent who skipped the upper layers is one of the easier matchups you'll face.

Pairing Primaries with Secondaries

  • Crit builds need crit resistance or hit rate, depending on the matchup. Against an opponent stacking dodge, you need hit rate to land your crits. Against an opponent stacking crit themselves, you need crit resistance to deny theirs. Then layer crit damage (passive sources) and damage increase (mechs) on top, fights end faster, which naturally counters healing-focused opponents.
  • Dodge builds need heal. Dodge alone slows fights down; heal is what keeps you alive through the hits that do land. The Gridstar + Ladybug + Berserk Dragon + Aqua Spirit defensive build in Part 2 is a clean example.
  • Burn builds (PVE) need crit (up to the Evil Eye ceiling) and crit damage. Once you're at the 100% burn cap, crit is the multiplier that pushes damage further, and Evil Eye routes overflow into burn crit, making crit damage do double duty.

On BP (Battle Power)

BP tells you how much someone has built. It doesn't tell you how well they've built it, or whether their build counters yours. A 30M BP player with a well-tuned consistency-and-scaling build can beat a 45M BP player whose investment is spread thin or focused on base stats, and lose to a 25M BP opponent who happens to counter their setup. Use BP as a rough screening signal, not an outcome predictor.

F2P Realities

You won't get to fully specialize every build f2p. High-tier sentinels, deep mech combination bonuses, and recast materials are the slowest to accumulate, and that's by design. Pick one or two builds you actually use and concentrate your resources there. A defensive PVP build and a PVE burn build is a reasonable starting pair. Spread yourself across three or four builds in parallel and you end up with all of them slightly underbuilt and none able to beat their natural counters. The chip formation system (three saved slots) already pushes you toward this, treat those slots as commitments, not experiments.

Pilots: The Foundation

Pilots drive four core combat stats: Physical Defense Break (PDB), Energy Defense Break (EDB), Physical Defense (PD), and Energy Defense (ED). Together they decide how much damage you deal and absorb. Skip pilots and you'll have a high BP and a hollow foundation that loses to comparably-resourced opponents who invested here.

Why Pilots Matter More Than They Look

  • You need to be close enough on these stats to compete. If your offense (PDB/EDB) and defense (PD/ED) are roughly comparable to opponents at your BP, you can win by exploiting an advantage in chips, drones, sentinels, mechs, or passives. If your pilots lag, you're hunting for a much bigger exploitable gap elsewhere, and against a well-built opponent that gap may not exist.
  • Pilots compound with everything else, so a player with mediocre gear but well-developed pilots is unusually tough for their BP. Starting fresh, I'd put pilots first.

The Equipped Pilot Bonus

Community Each pilot contributes specific stats passively based on their star level and awakening (not all four stats, just the ones that pilot provides). When you equip a pilot, their contribution to those stats jumps substantially above the passive value, which means swapping your equipped pilot can produce big swings in whichever stats they cover.

Example with Lancelot (an EDB/PD pilot):

  • 6-star SR Lancelot, sitting in your roster (passive): ~80 EDB and ~80 PD
  • 6-star SR Lancelot, equipped: ~320 EDB and ~320 PD, so about +240 to each stat over the passive value
  • Awakened Lancelot (the final upgrade past 6-star), equipped: about +300 to each stat over the passive value

Equip the pilot that covers the stats you most need for the matchup. Facing an energy opponent? Equip your highest-ED pilot and add +240 to +300 ED to your total. Facing a physical opponent? Highest-PD pilot, same math. The passive-vs-equipped delta is big enough that swapping before a fight can decide a close matchup. (This holds whether you're attacking or defending, both sides' stats matter in every battle.)

Don't Specialize Defensively

Most common pilot mistake: a physical-damage player maxes PDB and PD and lets EDB and ED lag. You don't get to pick what your opponent throws at you, and an energy build will tear through a defender who ignored ED. Max both PD and ED regardless of your offensive specialization.

Offense is more defensible to specialize, since you're choosing your own build, but maxing both PDB and EDB is still the long-term goal, some PVE encounters and PVP matchups reward the flexibility.

Defense Break vs. Defense: How the Math Works

Defense Break (your offensive stat) is compared against the opponent's Defense (their defensive stat). The difference between the two, not the raw values, drives a damage multiplier on top of everything else you've built. The same math applies in reverse on incoming damage: opponent's PDB vs. your PD, and opponent's EDB vs. your ED.

The Formula

If your Break ≥ enemy Defense (neutral or bonus zone):

Damage Multiplier = 1 + ((Your Break − Enemy Defense) / 1000)

If your Break < enemy Defense (penalty zone):

Damage Multiplier = 1 / (1 + ((Enemy Defense − Your Break) / 1000))

The only thing that matters is the gap: Your Break minus Enemy Defense. That gap places you in one of three zones: below (penalty), equal (neutral, 1.00x), or above (bonus). The same logic applies on the defensive side: when your PD exceeds an opponent's PDB, they are in their penalty zone against you.

The Breakpoint

The breakpoint is simple: Enemy Defense. Get to or above that number and the penalty zone disappears entirely. Below it, you are paying a multiplicative tax on every hit.

Why the Asymmetry Matters

The two formulas are not symmetric. Being above gives you a linear bonus; being below gives you an inverted penalty that compounds quickly. Worked examples at a few gap sizes:

GapAbove (Bonus)Below (Penalty)Effective Asymmetry
±1001.10x (+10%)1 / 1.10 = 0.909x (−9.1%)Small gaps are nearly symmetric
±2001.20x (+20%)1 / 1.20 = 0.833x (−16.7%)Gap starts to open
±5001.50x (+50%)1 / 1.50 = 0.667x (−33.3%)Penalty zone is meaningfully worse
±10002.00x (+100%)1 / 2.00 = 0.500x (−50%)Falling 1000 behind halves your damage

The worst place to be is the penalty zone. The first job of pilot investment is clearing the breakpoint against the opponents you fight. Once you're even or slightly above, you're paying no tax and your other systems work at full effectiveness. Pushing far above helps but has diminishing returns compared to climbing out of the penalty zone.

Worked Example: Even Match

Your Break = 1000, Enemy Defense = 1000 -> Multiplier = 1.00. You deal 100% of your base damage: no bonus, no penalty. This is the floor you want to hit before optimizing anywhere else.

PVP Burn Build

My current main PVP build. Regularly beats opponents 10M BP ahead, sometimes 15M+. One formation covers both of the dominant PVP archetypes:

  • Burn beats dodge. Burn stacks tick regardless of whether subsequent attacks land. Dodge opponents can duck your direct hits but not burn ticks already on them.

Burn in PVP (Mechanics Deep-Dive)

Burn ignores dodge. Stacks tick regardless of whether subsequent attacks land. Burn also scales with fight duration, exactly the kind of fight dodge/heal opponents want to extend. That's why burn is a direct dodge-build counter.

The 100% burn cap in PVP is net of opponent burn decrease. Against a 50% burn-reduce opponent, you need ~150% raw to deliver 100% net. Unlike PVE, there's no stopping point, keep stacking burn chips to overcome their decrease.

The anti-burn counter is contested. Two options:

  • Dodge / Burn Resistance (my pick). Dodge their regular hits, they can't crit you, and burn resistance negates the ticks. Covers all three damage sources at once.
  • Burn / Burn Decrease (Method's pick). Burn builds have their own sustain, dodge drags the fight out, and the longer it goes the more Star Envoy stacks build on them anyway. Burn can't be dodged, so a mirror pressure plus burn decrease is more direct.

The matchup likely depends on BP range and sentinel setup. If a specific burner is giving you trouble on Dodge / Burn Resistance, try the mirror.

If you run dodge or heal, you can't skip burn resistance. Burn ticks straight through dodge, and in long fights it climbs to meaningful values. Burn resistance (chips or engine) is the defensive piece a dodge or heal build cannot skip.

Part 3: Optimizing Your Builds

The technical reference. Each section below covers one system you'll tune when building: Chip Formations, Burn Drones, Sentinels, Mech Spirits, Engines, Camp Technology, and Titan Sets & Weapon Tech. Brief overviews first, then drill into the sections you need.

Understanding Your Tools

Chip Formations let you save up to three formations of stat-boosting chips. You assign chips to tailor your stats (burn, crit, dodge, hit rate, etc.) for different scenarios. Key constraints include the 100% cap on drone type enhancements (burning, electric, reflect) net of opponent resistance, and the 80% net cap on dodge and crit rate against players. In PVE, crit rate is capped at 80% as well, but the Evil Eye mech spirit allows you to convert excess crit into burn crit, which effectively extends the useful ceiling. See the Chip Formations section for details.

Sentinels are companion units (similar to drones) with up to four passive abilities at four stars. Passives come in three rarity tiers (gold, purple, and blue) and scale with star level, so a maxed low-rarity sentinel often outperforms a high-rarity sentinel at low stars. You equip one sentinel at a time. Unlike drone passives, sentinel passives stack across slots, meaning the same passive type appearing in multiple slots adds up. Note that you can't get two golds of the same passive on a single sentinel, but you can get a gold plus a purple plus a blue of the same type, which still produces a meaningful stacked bonus once the sentinel is fully leveled.

Mech Spirits provide a core skill and up to four attribute bonuses. You equip three mech spirits at a time and should swap them based on the situation. Beyond what you equip, your entire mech collection contributes passive bonuses based on the four attributes. In addition, you can get combination bonuses by accumulating and leveling mech spirits, there are 14 combinations with up to 22 levels each. You can advance the combination level when each mech in the combination has achieved a specified level itself. For example, to get to level 22 of the combination bonus, each of the relevant mechs must be at level 60.

Mech spirits have the most room to grow of any system covered in this guide. Especially early on, you can accumulate a tremendous amount of attribute bonus just by leveling mechs. The 14 combination bonuses extend that runway considerably beyond the mechs you personally equip.

Engines provide a stat boost that you select before battle. Each engine has a base bonus that grows as you level it, and the bonus doubles when equipped. For example, a grade 7 hit rate engine gives 35% hit rate passively and 70% when equipped.

How to Build Around a Strategy

A strong build layers all of these systems together to hit your caps efficiently. For a PVE burn build, you combine burn chips, mech combination bonuses (e.g., 22% passive burn from mechs alone), sentinel passives, and possibly Magic Armor to reach the 100% burn cap, then redirect everything else into crit, but only up to the ceiling that Evil Eye can absorb (more on this below). For PVP, remember that caps like 80% dodge are net values after your opponent's resistance. If your dodge is 160% and your opponent has 100% hit rate, your effective dodge is only 60%, so you still have room to grow. This means stacking dodge beyond 80% is not wasted if opponents have high hit rate. The same logic applies to crit rate in PVP.

Chip Formations

Chip formations give you three saved sets of stat chips, each tied to one of your underlying battle formations. You can swap between them before battles, but each set needs to align with the rest of your equipment for that formation (gems, gears, drones, sentinels, etc.). Most players will dedicate one formation to PVE and the other two to PVP.

The Evil Eye Conversion Ceiling (PVE)

Mechanic Crit rate in PVE is capped at 80% for direct weapon crits, same as PVP. Evil Eye runs on a parallel track: it converts your full crit rate (net of enemy crit resistance) into burn crit rate at a fraction of the original value. PVE bosses have no crit resistance, so your full raw crit rate feeds the conversion. The 80% cap applies only to weapon crits, not to the burn crit Evil Eye generates, so every additional point of crit is still producing more burn crit damage past 80% raw.

Mechanic The conversion rate improves substantially as Evil Eye levels. At Level 1, every 10% raw crit becomes 2% burn crit. At max level, every 10% raw crit becomes 5.6% burn crit. So the raw crit needed to produce the same burn crit drops as Evil Eye levels up.

Hard capCommunity There's also a cap on burn crit itself at ~80%. The 100% columns above are reference points for the raw conversion math; in practice the ceiling you're stacking toward is 80%, so the ~400% / ~143% numbers are the practical places to stop at the two ends of Evil Eye's range.

Track your burn crit stat as your stop point, not raw crit. Low Evil Eye leaves lots of room (keep stacking); maxed Evil Eye tightens the ceiling (redirect past ~143% raw crit toward crit damage, hit rate, or other stats).

If you don't run Evil Eye at all, the regular 80% PVE crit cap applies and there's no reason to push crit past that.

Building Toward the Burn Cap

Hard capMechanic The 100% cap on burn enhancement is always a net cap, raw burn minus opponent burn decrease. It never changes. What changes is how much raw burn you need to reach 100% net, depending on the matchup:
  • PVE: Bosses have no burn decrease, so 100% raw burn = 100% effective burn. Hard stop; past 100% raw does nothing.
  • PVP vs. opponent with no burn decrease: Same as PVE, 100% raw = 100% effective. Past 100% does nothing.
  • PVP vs. opponent with 50% burn decrease: Need 150% raw burn to deliver 100% effective. Burn chips past 150% start to over-shoot.
  • PVP vs. opponent with 100% burn decrease: Need 200% raw burn to deliver 100% effective. Still capped at 100% effective, just takes more raw to get there.

In short: PVE investment stops at 100% raw; PVP investment is about overcoming the opponent's reduction, not raising the cap. There's no stat gain for stacking raw burn above (100% + opponent's burn decrease).

For a burn-oriented PVE build, once you hit 100% burn from all sources combined (chips, sentinel passives, mech combination bonuses, equipped mech skills, Camp Tech), redirect remaining chip slots into crit. For PVP, keep stacking burn chips as long as your target opponents are running meaningful burn decrease.

Example from my setup: 22% burn enhancement from mech collection + ~20% from Magic Armor leaves ~60% to reach 100% raw from chips. That's the PVE finish line and the PVP starting line against opponents with burn resistance.

The Canonical Pairings

Over time our legion settled on a short list of chip-pair formations that cover the matchups you face. Mixing stats outside this list spreads your chips too thin and leaves you "average at everything, strong at nothing", the loss pattern that shows up most clearly in Star Scramble.

  • Dodge / Crit Resistance
  • Crit / Hit Rate
  • Burn Enhancement / Crit Resistance
  • Crit / Crit Resistance
  • Dodge / Hit Rate

You only run three of these at a time. The three you pick should match the roles your build needs to cover.

Formation Progression: Before, At, and After 100% Native Burn

The right three formations change as you progress. The key milestone is 100% native burn, 100% from mech combos, sentinel passives, equipped mech skills, and Camp Tech alone (no burn chips). Hitting it frees up an entire chip formation for PVP specialization, because your crit/hit formation now reaches the PVE burn cap on its own.

Preference

  • Stage 1, Below 100% native burn. You need a dedicated PVE burn formation to reach the cap. My three formations at this stage:
    • F1, Crit / Hit Rate: PVP offense
    • F2, Dodge / Crit Resistance: PVP defense
    • F3, Burn / Crit (PVE): burn and crit chips both collecting on this formation to reach 100% burn and push crit for Evil Eye conversion. When burn chips roll Crit Resistance as their secondary stat, hold onto them, they're the ones you'll want on F3 later when it converts to a dedicated PVP burn build.
  • Stage 2, At 100% native burn. Your crit/hit PVP formation now also reaches the PVE burn cap without any burn chips, so one of your three formation slots is freed. F3 becomes available for a dedicated PVP use. Common setup:
    • F1, Crit / Hit Rate: PVP offense (also doubles as your PVE formation now)
    • F2, Dodge / Crit Resistance: PVP defense
    • F3, Burn / Crit Resistance: PVP Burn Build. Burn chips are focused on overcoming opponent burn decrease (no longer needed to hit the PVE cap), with Crit Resistance as the secondary to deny opposing crit builds.
  • Stage 3, Past 100% native burn. Same three formations as Stage 2; your burn chips now stack higher raw burn (150%+ raw) because they're pushing past opponent burn resistance rather than reaching the PVE cap. This is the setup where PVP Burn Build can beat opponents 10M+ BP ahead.

Jumping to Stage 2 or 3 early backfires. Pulling burn chips off your PVE formation before native burn is at 100% drops your PVE output, and your PVP burn build is still too weak to carry fights, so you end up weaker in both modes. Grind the native burn stack first (mech combos, Camp Tech burn enhancement, sentinel burn passives), then transition.

Chip Placement Logic Across Formations

Formation 1 (Crit / Hit) gets first pick on crit chips that roll Hit as a strong secondary. If a crit chip has crit resistance instead (and that's useful on the matchups you expect), it goes on Formation 2 or 3 depending on what's weakest there. Burn chips go on Formation 3; the secondary doesn't matter much for the PVE version, but if the chip rolls crit resistance it's also useful for the anti-dodge PVP version.

Auto Mode Setup in the Chip Factory

The Chip Factory's Auto Mode lets you run continuous chip manufacturing with conditions that pause production when something useful comes out. I configure it separately for each of my three formations.

The key setting is "Stop when chip rating increases," which halts the factory whenever it produces a chip that would be an upgrade over what's currently equipped on the active formation. Combined with the "And" relationship between the two attribute rows, this gets tighter as the factory levels up, set and forget, tighten all the way. Attribute filters per formation:

  • Formation 1 (Crit / Hit): Crit Rate primary, Hit Rate secondary
  • Formation 2 (Dodge / Crit Resistance): Dodge Rate primary, Crit Resistance secondary
  • Formation 3 (Burn / Crit Resistance): Burning Enhancement and Crit Rate as the two primary rows (joined by "Or"); Crit Resistance as the secondary on the Burn row for anti-dodge PVP use

Formation 3 is the only formation where two different primary attributes are both actively useful, you're chasing 100% burn (PVE cap, PVP starting point) and loading up on crit after that for weapon-crit and drone-skill damage. Having both configured as primaries means the factory stops and lets you evaluate whenever it produces an upgrade in either category.

Raise your minimum chip-grade filter as the factory levels (e.g., Iridium 5+ only). Chip tiers surface progressively: Stardust 3, Aether, Quantum. Letting trash tiers through just slows your daily grind.

Dodge vs. Crit: When to Switch

The underlying principle: pick the primary your opponent is least equipped to counter. Hit Rate counters Dodge; Crit Resistance counters Crit. Favor Dodge against opponents with low Hit Rate, and favor Crit against opponents with low Crit Resistance. Both caps in PVP are net values (your stat minus opponent's counter), so raw stacking past 80% still produces returns when the opposing counter is high.

  • Opponent has low Crit Resistance? Favor Crit, their defense can't deny what you're throwing.
  • Opponent has low Hit Rate? Favor Dodge, net dodge stays high, so your chips do real work.
  • Opponent has high Dodge and high Hit Rate (hard to crit, hard to dodge)? Burn is often the right pick, it bypasses dodge entirely.

Separate from the primary pick: make sure your Hit Rate is high enough to land attacks against high-Dodge opponents. Hit Rate is a prerequisite for crit, not a substitute for the primary.

Burn Drones

Burn is one of the most flexible damage types in the game, strong in PVE against bosses, and useful in PVP because it bypasses dodge and scales over long fights (covered in Part 2). Choosing the right burn drones, and progressing them in the right order, is one of the higher-impact decisions you'll make.

Foundation: Two Maxed Ninjas

Start by maxing 1-2 Ninjas. They're cheap to level, and a maxed Ninja beats an under-leveled higher-rarity drone (same star-scaling logic that makes maxed Gridstars beat low-star SSRs). Two maxed Ninjas give you a working burn build today.

The R vs. SR Fork

After Ninjas, pick one path, not both:

  • R-tier: Faster to max, lower ceiling. Right for f2p and lightly-spending players. Options: Lich, Scorpion Lion, Cerberus.
  • SR-tier: Slower and more expensive, higher ceiling. SR burners (Sagittarius, Lyra, Fornax) outperform R drones once leveled. The most common f2p long-term endpoint is 2x maxed Ninja + 1 maxed SR. Heavy spenders stack multiple SRs.

The mistake is splitting investment across both tiers. You end up with under-leveled drones in both and no maxed lineup.

Choosing Among R-Tier Burn Drones

Lich is meaningfully stronger than Cerberus or Scorpion Lion. The reasoning generalizes to how to evaluate burn drones in general.

DroneEffectReal Value
Lich+15% to all burn damage, every burn tick, all the timeHighest. The bonus applies to every burn stack, every tick, across every burn drone you run, and is always active. It scales with everything else you stack.
CerberusSkill damage 255% + 3.5% per burn stack (max ~276% at 6 stacks)Modest. Roughly +8% to one drone's skill hit at full burn, not nothing, but it's a one-time bonus on a single drone where Lich is multiplying everything.
Scorpion Lion+1 burn stack when recoil is activeLower than it looks and getting worse over time. Burn caps at 6 stacks and you reach the cap within 2–3 cycles of attacks anyway; past cap the extra stack typically just replaces an old one (FIFO queue) rather than adding new damage. The passive is also conditional on recoil being active, a niche that mattered when recoil drones were common and shrinks further every patch cycle that recoil stays out of the meta.

The pattern: burn drones with continuous, apply-to-all-burn bonuses beat drones with conditional, capped, or single-hit bonuses. Lich is the first type; Cerberus and Scorpion Lion are the second.

Drones and Crit: What Actually Converts

Community Drone normal attacks don't participate in the global crit system. A drone's normal-attack crit rate is set by its Spot Weakness passive, period. Your global crit rate doesn't buff it; opponent crit resistance doesn't dampen it. No Spot Weakness = that drone never crits on normal attacks, which also means Raging Assault (crit damage multiplier) is wasted without Spot Weakness paired with it.

  • Evil Eye's crit → burn-crit conversion likely applies to drone skills and your weapon crit, not drone normal-attack ticks. Practical PVE conclusion is unchanged (stack crit anyway, it still boosts weapons and drone skills), but burn ticks from flaming status aren't amplified by Evil Eye the way weapon crits are.
  • Pair Spot Weakness with Raging Assault on any drone where you want normal-attack crits. Running one without the other is wasted investment.

Sentinels

Build a roster of sentinels with different passive combinations so you can swap between them to exploit specific weaknesses in your opponent's build.

Core Mechanics

Sentinels gain one passive at each star level, up to four passives at four stars. Each star level after 4 enhances one of the four passives (same as drones). Passives scale with underlying star level, so a maxed G-class sentinel (40 stars, ~12 days) often outperforms a well-rolled SSR that's only at a few stars. 18 passive types, each slot rolls from three rarity tiers: gold (9%), purple (18%), blue (~73%). The 9% gold rate is dramatically higher than drones' 0.42% per slot, which is what makes the rerolling strategy viable (see below).

Low-star passives aren't useless, they just hit much softer than at high stars. The payoff is maxing the sentinel so its passives scale fully.

G-class sentinels are the backbone of your early strategy because you can max several quickly and experiment with different passive combinations. You can evolve sentinels forever, you just get no additional benefit after 40 stars. You can also "reroll" their passives for the cost of one new sentinel, making them much cheaper and easier to reroll than drones (see below).

Recommended Sentinel Choices

Gridstar (G-class), Best Defensive Starter: Extremely cheap to get to max stars which ensures your passives are functioning at peak performance. At 40 stars, a gold heal passive provides a 100% heal benefit. Paired with Ladybug (mech spirit), this creates a guaranteed health recovery when you drop below 30% HP. At level 7, Ladybug restores 7.5% health, doubled to 15% by a gold heal at maximum power. If you also have the Cheat Death drone passive, the two effects stack for extreme survivability. While heal pairs very nicely with a Gridstar, expect to build multiple maxed Gridstars with different passives for flexibility.

Stormquill (G-class), Starter Alt for Crit Builds: A viable alternative to Gridstar for players building toward a crit-oriented build. The two cover different niches, Gridstar favors heal builds (and still works for crit); Stormquill is purpose-built for crit. If you know early that your main PVP build will be crit-focused rather than heal/dodge, Stormquill is a reasonable swap into your G-sentinel rotation.

Flashstrike (G-class), PVE / TC Meta: A G-sentinel that opens up a 2-second window with a 20% damage boost applied to all damage sources, drones included, on an 8-second cycle. That drone-amplifying effect is what makes it the preferred PVE sentinel for Titan Challenge and Legion Hunt. Target passives: 3x crit damage plus gold sentinel enhancement, this is a deliberate exception to the general PVE passive priorities (crit rate + crit damage + sent enhance), since Flashstrike's 2-second window makes stacked crit damage multiply during that burst more than mixed crit rate/crit damage would. Worth adding to your G-sentinel rotation once you're focused on PVE scaling.

SavagePlate (N-class), Best Offensive Starter: Its key advantage is a 2-second skill cooldown, the fastest available. The only other sentinel with a 2-second skill cooldown is Rendclaw (SSR). Most Star Scramble fights last under 6 seconds unless one or both players are running heal-centric defensive builds. Even in extended fights, the 2-second cooldown means you fire your skill slightly more often than sentinels on a 4- or 6-second cycle. Since SavagePlate is meant for offensive builds, you should generally expect shorter fights, but you still benefit from the faster cycle even when things go long. SavagePlate with a gold heal-reduction passive is also the specific counter sentinel against opponents running heal-stacked Gridstars.

SR-class (e.g., Arcglow, Glimshade): Reasonable mid-term targets, maxable in roughly 4–5 months. Personally planning to go with an Arcglow because heal enhancement is a benefit you can only get from sentinels. Glimshade is a reasonable alternative for crit builds, its heal effect only triggers against burning enemies, so it pairs naturally with a build that already equips at least one burn drone.

SSR-class (e.g., Doomblast, Rendclaw): Strictly long-term investments. Budget 1.5+ years to max unless spending heavily. The primary short-term reason to buy SSRs is the combination bonus (one of each). Only SavagePlate and Rendclaw share the 2-second skill cooldown. Doomblast fires every 4 seconds but its healing enhancement (+10%) stacks up to 5 times, giving it strong long-fight value once maxed.

I plan to skip R sentinels as there aren't any attractive options and it would only delay my progression to SR. I plan to build an SR (Arcglow) and SSR (Doomblast) in parallel, picking up stars opportunistically over the next 6-18 months.

Sentinel Investment Path

Recommended Progression

The progression depends on your build. There's no universal Gridstar -> SavagePlate -> SR -> SSR ladder, different builds take different paths. Pick based on your intended build:

  • Dodge / heal / burn builds: Max 2–3 Gridstars with varied passives (12 days each) -> build an SR like Arcglow (4–5 months) -> slowly build Doomblast as a long-term project. SavagePlate is optional on this path.
  • Crit-focused builds: Max 2–3 Stormquills or a SavagePlate (SavagePlate is a heavier investment but offers the 2-second cooldown) -> build Rendclaw as the long-term endgame SSR for crit-focused play.
  • PVE scaling: Max a Flashstrike early for Titan Challenge / Legion Hunt, it's a G-class so it maxes fast and its drone-amplifying damage boost is PVE meta.

Along the way, buy at least one of most sentinels to unlock the 9 combination bonuses (each bonus is a one-time unlock per sentinel). Buy one of each SSR for their combination bonus even if you don't plan to use them yet, those bonuses are permanent once earned.

Sentinel Passives and Rerolling

The Reverse Merge Technique

When you find a 1-star sentinel with a desirable gold passive, use it as the base and merge a higher-star sentinel into it. The 1-star keeps its gold passive, and the three other slots reroll. You effectively get a free reroll of three slots. The practical cost of a reverse-merge cycle is low, about 30 chips to buy the fresh 1-star sentinel.

How to do it: First, level down the higher-star sentinel to zero before merging it in, this is critical because leveling down returns overclock chips you'd otherwise burn. Then merge the higher-star sentinel into the 1-star base. You keep the one passive on your base sentinel, lose the four passives on the sentinel you merged in, and gain three new random passives. The merge still progresses the base sentinel toward max stars, so this isn't a free re-roll in a stars sense, you're also spending a sentinel copy's worth of progression. You can repeat the process indefinitely, even after hitting max stars.

Side sentinel strategy: Build one or more side sentinels. Get them to four stars and do a reverse merge as soon as you find a 1-star with your desired passive. Once you prefer the side sentinel's passives, merge your main into it. Low-risk way to chase upgrades without disrupting your current setup. I aim to have around half a dozen of these low 4+ star sentinels ready to go.

Sentinel Passives, Stacking and Rarity

Unlike drone passives, sentinel passives stack across slots: the same passive type appearing in multiple slots adds up. You can't get two golds of the same passive type on one sentinel, but you can have a gold, a purple, and a blue of the same type, and those will stack together for a meaningful combined bonus on a maxed sentinel. With a ~31.5% chance of rolling at least one gold across four slots, the rerolling system is generous enough that you can realistically pursue strong passive combinations at a relatively low cost in chips.

There are 18 different passive types and three levels of rarity for each passive, with increasing levels of benefit.

  • Arcglow (SR, sustain pick): Gold Heal Enhance, Purple Heal Enhance, Blue Heal Enhance, Gold Sentinel Enhance. Not a damage dealer, a time-extender for sustain builds and burn builds (burn needs long fights).
  • Glimshade (SR, hybrid): Gold Sentinel Enhance is the practical top pick (multiplier on the main skill); Gold Heal Enhance is a viable alternate. Requires at least one burn drone equipped for the heal to trigger.

  • Rendclaw (SSR, debuff / neutralizer): Gold Heal Reduce, Purple Heal Reduce, Gold Sentinel Enhance (for the active's own damage), Gold Sentinel Reduce. Do not put Gold Heal Enhance on a Rendclaw, it's a debuff sentinel, not a sustain sentinel.

Sentinel Enhancement is a multiplier on the primary attack/skill number only (not secondary skills), and it's multiplicative, not additive. A gold Sentinel Enhance at full strength takes a 440% skill to 440 × 1.75 = 770%. Attack and HP passives, by contrast, are flat values.

On SSR Sentinels

SSR passives are low-value for a long time because they scale with star level. Gold Sentinel Enhancement on a 3-star Rendclaw sounds great until you remember it's 37 purchases from max, and a well-rolled Gridstar or SavagePlate is outperforming it right now. Mech Spirits

Mech spirits have the most room to grow of any system in this guide. You equip three, and every mech in your collection contributes to passive combination bonuses whether equipped or not. 14 combinations, up to 22 levels each. The bonuses can be huge: Quantum Shift gives 2% dodge at level 1 and scales to 44% dodge at level 22. Each mech must hit a specified level to unlock the next combination tier (level 60 for the top). Buy lots of mech spirits and level them.

How Mech Leveling Works

Mech spirits follow a repeating 5-level cycle. At levels 1 through 4, you unlock or upgrade one attribute bonus slot each level (one slot at level 1, up to four at level 4). At level 5, the core skill upgrades. The cycle repeats: levels 6–9 upgrade attribute slots, level 10 upgrades the core skill again, and so on.

Mech Investment Strategy

Once your pilots are in good shape, shift spend toward mechs. There's a lot of cheap room to grow, and between the per-mech passive bonuses and the 14 combination bonuses (up to 22 levels each) the returns are big.

  • Rhino: 205% extra damage on sentinel skill releases + 5% burn enhancement stacking 5x at L2. Looks better on paper, doesn't outperform Magic Armor for me.
  • Crimson Shadow: 10% damage whole battle at L12. Less-rare alternative to Dragon Emperor, levels fast. Better for PVP where opponents drop below 60% HP mid-fight.
  • Fireball: 13.1% damage increase vs bosses at L20. Commonly available, levels fast, underperforms Dragon Emperor and Crimson Shadow despite looking better on paper.

Note: The Evil Eye and Star Envoy Synergy

SynergyEvil Eye and Star Envoy convert overflow stats into burn through parallel mechanics. Evil Eye routes overflow crit (above the 80% PVE cap) into burn crit. Star Envoy routes overflow burn enhancement (above the opponent's burn decrease) into reliable burn-damage stacks. In PVE, bosses have no burn decrease and no crit resistance, so both reach max effectiveness: every point of burn enhancement raises Star Envoy's trigger chance, and at 100% burn you get guaranteed triggers every third skill. The effects compound, Star Envoy's stacking burn-damage bonus amplifies the same burn ticks Evil Eye's burn crits are landing. Running both is what makes PVE crit-burn builds much stronger than burn-only.

Engines

You equip one engine before each battle. Each engine has a passive bonus that grows as you level it (~5% per upgrade), and the bonus doubles when equipped. Example: a grade 7 hit rate engine gives 35% hit rate passively and 70% when equipped.

Four of the five engines reduce specific incoming damage types (burn, electric, reflect) or counter crits (crit resistance). Hit rate is the odd one out, your offensive option, negating opponent dodge so your attacks land. Which engine to pick comes down to the matchup.

Quick Decision Rule

Preference On offense or unsure, Hit Rate, it's what makes the rest of your investments pay off. On Star Scramble defense, where fights span multiple attack attempts and heal/dodge can carry you through, I lean Crit Resistance to deny the big-damage spikes. Facing a known damage type? Pick the matching Decrease engine.

Camp Technology

Camp Tech is a separate progression track and one of the highest-impact stat sources in the game. Four tabs: Basic, Advanced, Top Tech, Super Tech. Top and Super Tech (added v1.7.800, April 2026) are where most build-defining investment lives.

Costs double every ~10 levels. BP inflation is a real risk, leveling unused stats converts diamonds into BP with no fight impact and hurts your matchmaking. Pick the stats you use, ignore the rest.

Recommended Priority Path

Final Damage first, then PVP-specific stats, then burn enhancement for burn builds.

  1. Community Final Damage Increase (FDI) and Final Damage Decrease (FDD). Multipliers applied at the end of the damage calc, scaling all your damage (weapons, drones, mechs) and all your incoming damage reduction. Non-linear penalty below 50 FDI, damage drops hard, so getting past 50 is top priority. For Super Tech, both FDD slots to L60 first.
  2. PVP Damage and PVP Damage Reduction. Core PVP scaling after FDI/FDD stabilize.
  3. Burn Enhancement (Top Tech). One of the paths to native 100% burn without using chip slots. I currently get about 9% burn from Camp Tech.
  4. Hit Rate, Crit Resistance, Dodge, Crit Rate (Top Tech, each to level 10, then cycle). Round out the stat profile after the big three above are in place.

Basic Tech attack boosts work like flat red-gem damage, which means they favor fast-firing weapons. Prioritize MG > WG > Missile for Basic Tech attack upgrades. Basic Tech also contains ~55.5k HP total, worth maxing.

Titan Sets & Weapon Tech

Pick your Titan set to match your damage type and the current Titan season, keep 5/5 so the set bonus doesn't drop, and invest in weapon tech in a disciplined order.

Titan Sets

  • Conqueror (physical). Default physical Titan gear set. Conqueror is also the name of a fighter class. The 5-piece set bonus requires flying the Conqueror fighter and equipping the Conqueror Titan set. When the guide says "Conqueror" in a build table under "Fighter," it means the fighter class; the Titan Sets section here refers to the gear set.
  • Destroyer (energy). The go-to energy set. Superior to Thor in every slot except the MG. Current Kraken Titan season rewards full Destroyer with +100% damage.
  • Thor (energy). Mostly underwhelming, "avoid at all costs" is the rule for most players. One exception: Thor MG performs well in the Arachne Titan season.

The 5/5 set bonus matters more than individual piece stats. Breaking the set to insert normal armor drops a 1.3x adjusted-attack bonus that applies to all three weapons and flows into your drones, sentinels, and mechs. Normal armor can deal damage in PVP battle logs, but giving up 5/5 for it is almost never worth it in Titan Challenge, and usually not in PVP either. Check the current Titan season before committing shards.

Weapon Tech Priorities

For a full-Destroyer energy PVP build, the Weapon Tech investment order (after Aerial +/- is maxed, see below) is:

  1. MG: base attack -> crit rate -> crit damage -> attack rate
  2. WG: same four, in the same order
  3. Missile: same four, in the same order

Keep MG, WG, Missile, and Shield at similar levels overall. MG and Missile get early priority because MG attack speed and the Inferno missile boost are high-impact.

Aerial +/- is higher priority than any of the above. Patch 1.7.820 (July 2026) extended aerial/ground/sea/boss damage attributes to apply to drones, mech spirits, and sentinels, not just weapons. Tyche's aerial passive, Ame-no-Uzume's aerial reduction, and the Legion Tech aerial slots all benefit. Prioritize both aerial increase and reduction to L10.

Legion Tech investment priorities for PVP: Sky Eagle Tech, Crystal Prism, and Eternal Pivot to level 80; everything else to level 60.

Prism winggun is the only winggun that auto-targets and fires behind you, making it the right PVE choice if you're not running full Titan gear.

Equipment Management

Downgrade excess gear to Rainbow only, lower tiers lose too much value in the melt. Keep a dummy set of extra pieces so overflow doesn't get consumed as blanks during upgrades. Only Titan + fighter set bonuses move the needle on PVP performance; normal gear pieces (Batarang, etc.) perform the same.

Military Rank is a special case. Unlike Legion Tech or Top Tech where unused stats produce BP bloat, Rank adds attack and HP that apply in every battle. Treat it as competitive BP, not padded BP.

Part 4: Resource & Event Economy

Build strategy is half the game. The other half is accumulating resources so when the right event lands you have the stockpile to use.

Daily Diamond Baseline

Minimum daily diamond spend to not fall behind:

  • Engine parts: 140💎
  • Chip factory parts: 100💎
  • Three daily challenge resources (AirSea Simulation for pilots, Critical Strike for drones, Sentinel Base for sentinels): 100💎 each, 300💎 total
  • Opportunistic daily shop buys: R pilot chests ~60💎, Alloy ~60💎, discounted Rhenite (≤90), discounted Recruitment Tickets (≤240)

You need ~540💎/day from Collection Center to break even. Below that, you're slowly bleeding progression.

The Weekly Event Cycle

Events rotate weekly: Mech, Pilot, Sentinel, Titan, Drone. Each pays tiered rewards for 100, 200, 300 purchases during the event window. Pulls only count if they happen in-event. Currency varies by event:

  • Mech week: 400 pulls for a full event cycle. Mech tickets are free pulls you've accumulated; you don't buy them. The paid alternative is diamonds: 300 per pull, or 2,600 for 10 pulls.
  • Pilot week: 300 pulls for a full cycle. Same model as mech, pilot tickets are free pulls. Diamonds alternative is also 300 per pull / 2,600 per 10.
  • Drone week: 8,000 Rhenite per round. Rhenite is purchasable in the Resource tab at 1 per 10 diamonds, so a round effectively costs up to 80,000 diamonds if you buy all the way to the cap (but see the drone-event section below, you only pay 20% of that in practice).
  • Sentinel week: 8,000 sentinel chips per round. Same 1 per 10 diamonds in the Resource tab.

The core rule: save event-specific currency for the matching week, since the tiered rewards only pay out during the event.

The Drone-Event Buy/Sell Technique

Each round looks like it costs 8,000 Rhenite, but you only lose about 1,600. You can run the event up to 4 times a month, which pays out huge.

How the 8,000 Rhenite works

Each round asks for 8,000 Rhenite in drone purchases before rewards unlock. During the event you can sell any drone back for 80% of what you paid. Buy an SR for 1,000 Rhenite, sell immediately, net cost 200. Keep cycling until total purchases hit 8,000. Real cost: 1,600 Rhenite per round (20%). Buy and sell as fast as possible.

Modified chips

Chips are an alternative payment, 2 Rhenite each toward the 8,000, spent by recasting drones (re-rolling passives). Only recast during this event, it's one of the best chip sinks in the game. Catch: chips don't refund. Once spent, gone.

Running out of Rhenite?

Buy Rhenite with diamonds at 10 💎/Rhenite. Pricey, but the event pays enough diamonds that it's often worth it.

Rewards

Per round: 5,800 💎, 800 chips, 4 SR chests. 8 chests fuses one selectable SR drone (you pick, no roll). Full 4 rounds: 23,200 💎, 3,200 chips, 16 chests = two selectable SR drones.

Rules of thumb

  • Save all Rhenite between events. Buying outside the event window skips the event-point multiplier.
  • Keep only the drone(s) you're building. Combination bonuses are permanent once earned; the game remembers each drone's highest evolution, so you can max → sell → max another and still accumulate trio bonuses.
  • Plan before the event ends. I built a drone-event calculator at drone-calc-nu.vercel.app to work out how many rounds your current Rhenite + chip stockpile covers.

Pilot-Event Free Pulls

Free-pull sources during pilot weeks: 2/day baseline + 3/day from Endless Voyage + 1 from daily missions. Daily discipline (missions + a full 10-level Endless Voyage run) compounds over months. The $4.99 pilot monthly pack and the Legion Warfare pass are the best paid value for badges + tickets. Badges are typically the bottleneck for pilot progression (~10,000 badges ≈ ~125 days without events).

To hit the 300-pull reward tier without diamonds, bank ~225 tickets before the event starts. Below ~200 tickets, skip the event, you won't hit a reward tier worth the diamond spend.

Sentinel Event: Every Other Month

The sentinel event rewards purchases only, recasting, fusing, and leveling don't count. It runs monthly, but for most players the right cadence is every other month (unless you're spending diamonds to cover the gap). Skip one month to accumulate chips, then run the full event the next month to collect the free selectable SSR chest. Monthly participation is wasteful because ticket income doesn't keep up with per-event ticket cost. Legion Clash ranking also awards free SSRs, lowering the real SSR cost well below the 38,000-ticket raw: ~34k with free refreshes, ~28k counting Legion Clash drops.
